QUERY 1:
  "What are the three requirements for a security interest to attach?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  A security interest "attaches" to collateral -- meaning it becomes
  enforceable against the debtor -- when three conditions are met:

  1. **Value has been given** -- The secured party has provided something of
     value to the debtor (usually a loan, line of credit, or goods on credit).

  2. **The debtor has rights in the collateral** -- The debtor owns or has
     sufficient rights in the property to grant a security interest.

  3. **A security agreement exists** -- Either a written (or electronic)
     security agreement authenticated by the debtor that describes the
     collateral, OR the secured party has possession or control of the
     collateral (a "pledge").
     [Source: ucc_article9_guide.md, Chunk 3]

QUERY 2:
  "Where do I file a UCC-1 for an LLC formed in Delaware that operates in
   California?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  For registered organizations (corporations, LLCs, LPs, etc.), you file the
  UCC-1 with the Secretary of State in the state where the organization was
  **formed** (state of incorporation or formation), NOT the state where the
  organization does business or where the collateral is located.
  [Source: state_filing_handbook.md, Chunk 1]

  Therefore, for a Delaware LLC operating in California, you would file the
  UCC-1 with the **Delaware Division of Corporations** (Delaware Secretary
  of State). The filing fee in Delaware is $50. Delaware also offers premium
  expedited services including 1-hour turnaround for $1,000.
  [Source: state_filing_handbook.md, Chunk 2]
  [Source: filing_procedures_faq.md, Chunk 4]

QUERY 3:
  "How is a tractor classified as collateral under Article 9? Does it
   depend on who owns it?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  Yes, the classification of a tractor (or any good) under Article 9 depends
  on the debtor's use of the item, not on the item itself. The same tractor
  can be classified differently depending on context:

  - If the debtor is a **farm operating company** using it for farming, it is
    classified as **Farm Products** (supplies used in farming operations)
  - If the debtor is a **construction company** using it in business, it is
    classified as **Equipment** (goods used in a business that are not
    inventory or farm products)
  - If the debtor is a **tractor dealership** holding it for sale, it is
    classified as **Inventory** (goods held for sale)
  - If the debtor is an **individual homeowner** using it for personal
    purposes, it is classified as **Consumer Goods**
  [Source: collateral_classification.md, Chunk 4]

  Equipment is the "residual" category for business goods -- if goods don't
  fit the other three categories (inventory, farm products, consumer goods),
  they are classified as equipment.
  [Source: collateral_classification.md, Chunk 2]

QUERY 4:
  "I missed the continuation window and my filing lapsed. What happens now?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  If your filing has lapsed (the 5-year period expired without a continuation
  being filed), the consequences are significant:

  1. The security interest is now **unperfected** as to third parties
  2. You CANNOT revive the lapsed filing -- a new UCC-1 must be filed
  3. The new filing's priority date will be the date of the NEW filing
     (you lose your original priority position)
  4. Other creditors who filed or perfected between the lapse and the new
     filing will have priority
  5. A bankruptcy trustee may be able to avoid the security interest if the
     lapse occurred within the preference period (90 days before bankruptcy
     filing)
  [Source: filing_procedures_faq.md, Chunk 10]

  **Prevention:** Set up a tickler/calendar system to flag filings 12 months,
  6 months, and 3 months before lapse. Many lenders use automated portfolio
  management systems to track continuation deadlines.
  [Source: filing_procedures_faq.md, Chunk 10]

  For reference, the continuation window is the 6-month period before lapse
  (between 4.5 and 5 years after the original filing). A continuation filed
  before this window is ineffective.
  [Source: ucc_article9_guide.md, Chunk 6]
