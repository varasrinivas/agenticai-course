# Clinical Policy: Cardiac Catheterization and Coronary Angiography

**Policy Number:** CP-CARD-2024-005
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2024
**Last Reviewed:** November 15, 2023
**Category:** Cardiology — Invasive Procedures
**LOB:** Commercial, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid Managed Care

---

## 1. Policy Statement

This policy defines the medical necessity criteria for diagnostic cardiac
catheterization, coronary angiography, and related hemodynamic assessments.
Prior authorization is required for elective (non-emergent) cardiac
catheterization performed in an outpatient or inpatient setting.

**Emergency cardiac catheterization for acute STEMI (ST-elevation myocardial
infarction) does not require prior authorization.** Notification within 48 hours
of the procedure is required for retrospective review.

## 3. Medical Necessity Criteria

### 3.1 Diagnostic Coronary Angiography — Indications

Coronary angiography is medically necessary when ANY of the following clinical
scenarios are present:

**A. Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS):**
- ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) — ICD-10: I21.0 - I21.3
  - Emergency catheterization within 90 minutes of presentation (door-to-balloon time)
  - No prior authorization required; retrospective notification within 48 hours
- Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) — ICD-10: I21.4
  - Early invasive strategy (within 24 hours) for high-risk features: TIMI risk score >= 5, dynamic ST changes, elevated troponin, hemodynamic instability
  - Delayed invasive strategy (24-72 hours) for intermediate-risk patients: TIMI 3-4 without high-risk features
- Unstable angina — ICD-10: I20.0
  - With objective evidence of ischemia (positive stress test, dynamic ECG changes)
  - Refractory to anti-ischemic medical therapy

**B. Stable Ischemic Heart Disease (SIHD):**
- Positive non-invasive stress testing indicating moderate to severe ischemia:
  - Nuclear perfusion imaging: >= 10% ischemic myocardium
  - Stress echocardiography: >= 3 segments with new wall motion abnormality
  - Exercise ECG: >= 2mm ST depression, positive at < 5 METs, or associated with hypotension
  - Coronary CT angiography (CCTA): >= 50% stenosis in left main or proximal LAD, or >= 70% stenosis in any major epicardial vessel
- Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) Class III-IV angina despite optimal medical therapy for at least 4 weeks
- Angina with reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF < 50%) of unclear etiology

**C. Preoperative Evaluation:**
- Prior to valve surgery in patients aged >= 40 years or with cardiac risk factors
- Prior to non-cardiac surgery ONLY when non-invasive testing suggests high-risk coronary anatomy AND the results would change surgical management
- Prior to heart transplant listing (mandatory per UNOS criteria)

**D. Heart Failure Evaluation:**
- New-onset heart failure (LVEF < 40%) of unknown etiology to evaluate for ischemic cardiomyopathy
- Cardiomyopathy with chest pain or anginal equivalent suggesting ischemic component
- Cardiomyopathy with positive non-invasive ischemia testing

**E. Valvular Heart Disease:**
- Severe aortic stenosis being evaluated for surgical or transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR)
- Severe mitral regurgitation being evaluated for surgical repair/replacement or transcatheter repair (MitraClip)
- Symptomatic valvular disease with concern for concurrent coronary artery disease

### 3.2 Right Heart Catheterization — Indications

Right heart catheterization is medically necessary for:

- Evaluation of pulmonary hypertension:
  - Elevated estimated pulmonary artery systolic pressure on echocardiography (>= 40 mmHg)
  - Unexplained dyspnea with echocardiographic findings suggestive of pulmonary hypertension
  - Required for WHO classification and to guide PAH-specific therapy initiation
- Assessment of hemodynamic status in:
  - Advanced heart failure for transplant or mechanical circulatory support evaluation
  - Cardiogenic shock (may be performed without prior authorization)
  - Constrictive pericarditis vs. restrictive cardiomyopathy differentiation
  - Significant valvular disease to quantify severity and assess hemodynamic impact
- Post-transplant surveillance endomyocardial biopsy (protocol-driven, typically does not require PA)

### 3.3 Repeat / Follow-up Catheterization

Repeat coronary angiography is medically necessary when:

- New or worsening symptoms after prior revascularization (PCI or CABG):
  - Recurrent angina within 9 months of PCI (evaluate for in-stent restenosis)
  - New angina after CABG (evaluate graft patency)
- Positive non-invasive testing after prior revascularization indicating new ischemia
- Planned staged PCI (second procedure for multi-vessel disease):
  - Typically within 30-90 days of the index PCI
  - Must be documented in the original procedural report
- Heart transplant coronary allograft vasculopathy (CAV) surveillance: annually per ISHLT guidelines

## 4. Non-Covered Indications

Cardiac catheterization is NOT medically necessary for:

- Routine screening in asymptomatic patients without objective evidence of ischemia
- Repeat catheterization within 12 months without new symptoms, clinical change, or interval non-invasive testing
- Stable angina adequately controlled on medical therapy without high-risk non-invasive test results
- Assessment of coronary disease when non-invasive testing has not been performed (except in ACS, high clinical suspicion with contraindications to non-invasive testing, or pre-operative evaluation for valve surgery)
- Pre-operative evaluation for low-risk non-cardiac surgery
- Evaluation of atypical chest pain with negative or low-risk non-invasive workup and low pre-test probability (< 15% by validated risk calculator)

## 5. Clinical Documentation Requirements

The requesting provider must include:

1. **Clinical history:** presenting symptoms, duration, severity, and progression
2. **Physical examination findings:** relevant cardiovascular exam findings
3. **ECG:** 12-lead ECG performed within the last 30 days (or at presentation for ACS)
4. **Non-invasive testing results:** complete report of stress test, echocardiogram, CCTA, or nuclear imaging performed within the last 6 months
5. **Current medications:** complete list of cardiac medications including doses and duration
6. **Lab results:** troponin (for ACS), BNP/NT-proBNP (for heart failure), basic metabolic panel, CBC
7. **Prior catheterization reports:** if repeat catheterization is requested
8. **Risk factor profile:** hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, smoking status, family history of premature CAD

## 6. Appropriate Use Criteria (AUC) Compliance

As of January 1, 2024, CMS requires ordering providers to consult an approved
Appropriate Use Criteria Clinical Decision Support Mechanism (AUC-CDSM) before
ordering advanced cardiac imaging or invasive procedures.

- The AUC consultation must be documented with the order
- Qualified CDSM vendors include: AIM Specialty Health, eviCore, CareCore National
- AUC rating of "Appropriate" or "May Be Appropriate" is required for authorization
- AUC rating of "Rarely Appropriate" requires additional clinical justification and peer-to-peer review

## 7. Facility and Provider Requirements

- Cardiac catheterization must be performed at a facility with:
  - On-site cardiac surgery capability (Class I recommendation) OR
  - Established transfer agreement with a cardiac surgery center (acceptable for diagnostic-only procedures)
- Performing physician must be board-certified or board-eligible in interventional cardiology
- Facility must participate in a national quality registry (e.g., NCDR CathPCI Registry)

## 8. Complications and Adverse Events

Known risks that should be discussed during informed consent:

- Vascular access complications: hematoma (2-6%), pseudoaneurysm (0.5-2%), AV fistula (< 1%)
- Coronary dissection (0.1-0.4%)
- Stroke (0.1-0.3%)
- Contrast nephropathy (2-7%, higher in patients with CKD or diabetes)
- Radiation exposure: typical fluoroscopy time 5-15 minutes, dose area product varies
- Mortality: overall < 0.1% for diagnostic catheterization
