# Clinical Policy: Outpatient Physical Therapy and Rehabilitation Services

**Policy Number:** CP-REHAB-2024-007
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2024
**Last Reviewed:** December 10, 2023
**Category:** Rehabilitation Services
**LOB:** Commercial, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid Managed Care

---

## 1. Policy Statement

This policy establishes the medical necessity criteria, visit limits, and
authorization requirements for outpatient physical therapy (PT), occupational
therapy (OT), and speech-language pathology (SLP) services. This policy
applies to services rendered in outpatient rehabilitation facilities, physical
therapist private practices, and hospital outpatient departments.

## 3. Authorization Requirements

### 3.1 Authorization Tiers

**Tier 1 — No Prior Authorization Required:**
- Initial evaluation (97161-97163, 97165-97167): 1 visit
- Post-surgical rehabilitation for the first 12 visits within 8 weeks of surgery, for the following procedures:
  - Total knee arthroplasty (CPT 27447)
  - Total hip arthroplasty (CPT 27130)
  - ACL reconstruction (CPT 29888)
  - Rotator cuff repair (CPT 23412)
  - Lumbar or cervical spinal fusion (CPT 22551, 22612, 22630)
  - Total shoulder arthroplasty (CPT 23472)

**Tier 2 — Concurrent Review (authorization at visit 13+):**
- Continuation beyond the initial 12 visits
- Requires submission of treatment plan, progress notes, and functional outcome data
- Authorized in blocks of 6-12 visits
- Maximum initial authorization period: 8 weeks

**Tier 3 — Prior Authorization Required (from visit 1):**
- Non-surgical conditions that do not fall under Tier 1
- Chronic pain management programs
- Aquatic therapy / pool therapy
- Work hardening / work conditioning programs
- Vestibular rehabilitation
- Lymphedema management (complex decongestive therapy)
- Pelvic floor rehabilitation

### 3.2 Visit Limits by Plan Type

| Plan Type | PT Annual Visit Limit | OT Annual Visit Limit | SLP Annual Visit Limit |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Commercial PPO | 60 visits | 60 visits | 60 visits |
| Commercial HMO | 30 visits | 30 visits | 30 visits |
| Medicare Advantage | Per Medicare therapy cap policy (KX modifier threshold: $2,330 for PT/SLP combined, $2,330 for OT) |
| Medicaid Managed Care | 20 visits per authorization, up to 60 per year with continued medical necessity |

## 4. Medical Necessity Criteria

### 4.1 General Criteria (must be met for ALL rehabilitation services)

**A. Skilled Care Requirement:**
- Services must require the skills of a licensed therapist
- The complexity of the condition or treatment must be such that services can only be safely and effectively performed by or under the supervision of a qualified therapist
- Maintenance therapy (services that can be performed by the patient independently or by a caregiver) is NOT covered unless skilled oversight is needed to maintain function in the context of a complex medical condition

**B. Reasonable and Necessary:**
- Treatment must be directed toward a specific, documented functional goal
- Goals must be measurable, time-bound, and attainable within the authorization period
- Treatment intensity and frequency must be appropriate to the condition
- Typical frequency: 2-3 times per week for most conditions; 1 time per week for maintenance-level care
- Duration: typically 4-12 weeks for most musculoskeletal conditions

**C. Documentation Requirements:**
- Initial evaluation documenting: diagnosis, medical history, current functional status, objective measurements (ROM, strength, pain level), functional limitations, treatment goals, and plan of care
- Progress notes for each visit documenting services rendered, time spent, patient response, and progress toward goals
- Re-evaluation every 10 visits or 30 days (whichever comes first) with updated objective measurements and goal status

### 4.2 Condition-Specific Criteria

**Musculoskeletal Conditions (ICD-10: M00-M99):**
- Low back pain (M54.5): Initial trial of 6-8 visits over 4 weeks; continuation requires documented functional improvement of at least 10% on validated outcome measure (Oswestry Disability Index, Roland-Morris)
- Shoulder impingement (M75.1): Up to 12 visits; must document ROM improvement and functional gains
- Knee osteoarthritis (M17.x): Up to 12 visits; focus on strengthening and functional mobility; continuation beyond 12 visits requires documentation of progressive improvement
- Cervical radiculopathy (M54.12): Up to 12 visits; must include neurodynamic assessment and functional outcome tracking

**Post-Surgical Rehabilitation:**
- Total knee arthroplasty: up to 24 visits over 12 weeks; expected milestones:
  - Week 2: ROM 0-90 degrees, independent transfers
  - Week 6: ROM 0-110 degrees, ambulation without assistive device indoors
  - Week 12: ROM 0-120 degrees, return to community ambulation, stair negotiation
- ACL reconstruction: up to 36 visits over 6 months; phased protocol:
  - Phase 1 (Weeks 0-2): ROM, quad activation, gait training
  - Phase 2 (Weeks 2-6): Progressive strengthening, balance training
  - Phase 3 (Weeks 6-12): Functional training, sport-specific activities
  - Phase 4 (Months 3-6): Return-to-sport progression, agility, plyometrics
- Rotator cuff repair: up to 24 visits over 16 weeks; progression per surgeon protocol (typically includes immobilization phase, passive ROM, active ROM, strengthening phases)

**Neurological Conditions (ICD-10: G00-G99, I60-I69):**
- Stroke rehabilitation (I63.x): up to 36 visits over 12 weeks in the outpatient setting; continuation requires documented functional improvement on Functional Independence Measure (FIM) or equivalent
- Parkinson's disease (G20): up to 12 visits per authorization; LSVT BIG or PWR! program preferred; re-authorization every 12 weeks based on functional status
- Multiple sclerosis (G35): up to 12 visits per exacerbation; focus on functional maintenance and fall prevention during relapse; reauthorization for new exacerbations or functional decline
- Traumatic brain injury (S06.x): up to 36 visits over 12 weeks; multidisciplinary approach recommended (PT + OT + SLP); continuation based on measurable cognitive and physical gains

**Vestibular Rehabilitation:**
- Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) (H81.1): 1-4 visits for canalith repositioning; additional visits for persistent symptoms
- Vestibular neuritis / labyrinthitis (H81.2, H83.0): up to 12 visits; habituation and gaze stabilization exercises
- Must include documented Dizziness Handicap Inventory (DHI) score at baseline and at discharge

### 4.3 Discharge Criteria

Therapy services should be discontinued when ANY of the following are met:

- Patient has achieved all stated treatment goals
- Patient has reached a functional plateau (no measurable improvement over 2 consecutive re-evaluation periods)
- Patient is non-compliant with the treatment plan (missed >= 3 consecutive scheduled visits without rescheduling, or documented non-adherence to home exercise program)
- Condition has resolved or improved to the point where skilled therapy is no longer required
- Patient can independently perform the home exercise program and self-management strategies

## 5. Provider Requirements

### 5.1 Qualified Providers
- Licensed Physical Therapist (PT, DPT)
- Licensed Occupational Therapist (OTR/L)
- Licensed Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP, CCC-SLP)
- Physical Therapy Assistant (PTA) — under the supervision of a licensed PT, per state practice act
- Occupational Therapy Assistant (COTA) — under the supervision of a licensed OT, per state practice act

### 5.2 Supervision Requirements
- Direct supervision required for PTAs and COTAs per CMS guidelines and state practice acts
- Evaluations and re-evaluations must be performed by the licensed therapist, not the assistant
- The supervising therapist must co-sign all treatment notes for services rendered by assistants

### 5.3 Billing Guidelines
- Time-based codes (97110, 97112, 97116, 97140, 97530): bill per 15-minute unit, per the 8-minute rule
  - 8-22 minutes = 1 unit
  - 23-37 minutes = 2 units
  - 38-52 minutes = 3 units
  - 53-67 minutes = 4 units
- Maximum billable units per session: typically 4-6 units (60-90 minutes of skilled therapy)
- Hot/cold packs (97010) cannot be billed as the sole service for a visit
- Group therapy (97150): maximum 1 therapist to 4 patients; reduced reimbursement rate

## 6. Utilization Management Criteria for Continued Stay

At each concurrent review, the following will be evaluated:

1. **Progress toward goals:** Is the patient making measurable progress (>= 10% improvement on validated outcome measure per review period)?
2. **Skilled care necessity:** Does the current treatment plan require the expertise of a licensed therapist?
3. **Realistic timeline:** Are the remaining goals achievable within the requested authorization period?
4. **Treatment frequency appropriateness:** Is the requested frequency consistent with the diagnosis and phase of recovery?
5. **Home exercise program:** Has a HEP been established and is the patient compliant?
6. **Discharge planning:** Is there a documented discharge plan with anticipated discharge date?

## 7. Non-Covered Services

- Massage therapy performed by a massage therapist (not a licensed PT performing manual therapy)
- Gym memberships or fitness programs
- Hot tub or spa treatments
- Services that are solely for the purpose of maintaining the patient's current level of function without documented need for skilled intervention
- Services delivered via telehealth for conditions requiring hands-on manual therapy techniques
- Duplicate services: receiving the same therapy type from 2 different providers concurrently without documented clinical justification

## 8. Telehealth Policy

Outpatient rehabilitation services may be provided via telehealth (synchronous audio-video) for:

- Exercise instruction and home exercise program progression
- Neuromuscular re-education (balance, coordination) — with appropriate safety measures
- Therapeutic activities and self-care training
- Patient education and self-management strategies
- Re-evaluations when objective measurements can be obtained remotely

Telehealth is NOT appropriate for:
- Manual therapy techniques
- Electrical stimulation or ultrasound
- Gait training requiring hands-on assistance
- Initial evaluations for complex neurological conditions
