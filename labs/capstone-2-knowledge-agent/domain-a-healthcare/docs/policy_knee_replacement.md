# Clinical Policy: Total and Partial Knee Arthroplasty (Knee Replacement)

**Policy Number:** CP-ORTH-2024-003
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2024
**Last Reviewed:** November 30, 2023
**Category:** Orthopedic Surgery
**LOB:** Commercial, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid Managed Care

---

## 1. Policy Statement

This policy defines the medical necessity criteria for total knee arthroplasty
(TKA), partial (unicompartmental) knee arthroplasty (UKA), and revision knee
arthroplasty. Prior authorization is required for all elective knee replacement
procedures. Emergency or urgent cases may receive retrospective review.

## 3. Medical Necessity Criteria for Total Knee Arthroplasty (CPT 27447)

### 3.1 Required Clinical Criteria (ALL must be met)

Total knee arthroplasty is medically necessary when ALL of the following
criteria are satisfied:

**A. Radiographic Evidence:**
- Weight-bearing AP and lateral knee radiographs demonstrating moderate to severe
  osteoarthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence Grade 3 or 4), defined as:
  - Grade 3: Definite osteophytes, moderate joint space narrowing, some sclerosis, possible deformity
  - Grade 4: Large osteophytes, marked joint space narrowing, severe sclerosis, definite deformity
- Radiographs must be dated within the last 12 months

**B. Functional Impairment:**
- Documented functional limitation in at least 2 of the following activities of daily living:
  - Walking (limited to less than 2 blocks or requiring assistive device)
  - Stair climbing (unable to ascend/descend without significant pain or support)
  - Rising from a seated position (requires use of arms or assistance)
  - Activities of daily living (dressing, bathing, personal hygiene)
- Validated outcome score recommended: WOMAC score >= 39/96 or KOOS Pain subscale <= 50/100

**C. Pain Severity:**
- Moderate to severe knee pain (NRS >= 5/10) present for at least 3 months
- Pain is refractory to conservative management (see Section 3.2)
- Pain significantly impacts sleep, work, or quality of life (documented in clinical notes)

**D. Body Mass Index (BMI):**
- BMI < 40 kg/m2 preferred
- BMI 40-45 kg/m2: may be approved with documentation of obesity-related surgical risk mitigation plan AND orthopedic surgeon attestation that benefits outweigh risks
- BMI > 45 kg/m2: not routinely authorized; requires peer-to-peer review and documentation of why surgery cannot be deferred until BMI reduction is achieved

### 3.2 Required Conservative Management (minimum 3 months)

Documentation of trial and failure or intolerance of at least 3 of the following
5 conservative treatments:

1. **Pharmacotherapy** — Trial of at least 2 of the following:
   - Acetaminophen (up to 3000mg/day for at least 4 weeks)
   - Oral NSAIDs (at least 2 different agents, each for at least 4 weeks)
   - Topical NSAIDs (at least 4 weeks)
   - Duloxetine (for patients with contraindications to NSAIDs)

2. **Physical Therapy** — Supervised physical therapy program:
   - Minimum of 6 visits over at least 6 weeks
   - Must include quadriceps strengthening and range-of-motion exercises
   - Documentation of attendance and progress (or lack thereof)

3. **Intra-articular Injections** — At least 1 of the following:
   - Corticosteroid injection (maximum 3 per year in the affected knee)
   - Hyaluronic acid viscosupplementation (completed full series)

4. **Activity Modification and Weight Management:**
   - Documented counseling on activity modification
   - Weight loss counseling or program referral if BMI >= 30

5. **Assistive Devices:**
   - Trial of unloader knee brace, cane, or walker
   - Documentation of compliance and inadequate symptom relief

### 3.3 Exceptions to Conservative Management Requirement

Conservative management is NOT required when:

- Acute fracture involving the knee joint requiring arthroplasty
- Septic arthritis of the knee requiring emergent intervention
- Tumor involving the distal femur or proximal tibia requiring resection
- Post-traumatic arthritis with joint instability preventing safe ambulation
- Failed prior osteotomy with progressive deformity

## 4. Medical Necessity Criteria for Unicompartmental Knee Arthroplasty (CPT 27446)

### 4.1 Patient Selection Criteria (ALL must be met)

- Isolated unicompartmental disease (medial or lateral compartment only)
- Intact anterior cruciate ligament (ACL)
- Correctable varus or valgus deformity (< 15 degrees)
- Flexion contracture < 10 degrees
- Flexion range of motion >= 90 degrees
- BMI < 35 kg/m2 (relative contraindication above this threshold)
- Age >= 40 years (relative criterion — younger patients should be evaluated for osteotomy)

### 4.2 Contraindications for UKA
- Inflammatory arthritis (rheumatoid, psoriatic, etc.)
- Bicompartmental or tricompartmental disease
- ACL deficiency
- Fixed varus/valgus deformity > 15 degrees
- Prior high tibial osteotomy (relative contraindication)

## 5. Revision Knee Arthroplasty (CPT 27486, 27487)

### 5.1 Indications for Revision

- Aseptic loosening of one or more components confirmed by imaging
- Periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) confirmed by:
  - Elevated serum ESR and CRP, AND
  - Positive synovial fluid culture or cell count (WBC > 3000 cells/uL, PMN% > 80%)
- Instability with recurrent subluxation or dislocation
- Polyethylene wear with measurable osteolysis
- Periprosthetic fracture (Vancouver classification)
- Component malposition causing pain or limited function
- Stiffness / arthrofibrosis (ROM < 70 degrees) refractory to manipulation under anesthesia

### 5.2 Timing Requirements

- Revision for aseptic loosening: generally not approved within 2 years of index procedure unless clear radiographic progression
- Two-stage revision for PJI: minimum 6 weeks of IV antibiotics between explant and reimplantation (per MSIS/ICM criteria)

## 6. Perioperative Requirements

### 6.1 Pre-operative Optimization

- Hemoglobin A1c < 8.0% for diabetic patients (within 3 months of surgery)
- Active tobacco use: smoking cessation counseling documented; ideally 4+ weeks cessation pre-op
- Dental clearance recommended to reduce infection risk
- Preoperative medical clearance by PCP or cardiologist if age >= 65 or significant comorbidities
- Nutritional optimization: albumin >= 3.5 g/dL, prealbumin >= 15 mg/dL

### 6.2 Site of Service

- TKA may be performed in hospital inpatient or ambulatory surgical center (ASC)
- ASC setting appropriate for patients meeting ALL of the following:
  - ASA class I or II
  - BMI < 35
  - No significant cardiac or pulmonary disease
  - Adequate home support for same-day or next-day discharge
  - Age < 75 (relative criterion)

## 7. Post-operative Follow-up and Rehabilitation

- Physical therapy to begin within 24-48 hours post-operatively
- Outpatient PT: minimum 2x/week for 6-12 weeks
- DVT prophylaxis per AAOS guidelines (minimum 2 weeks, typically 4 weeks)
- Follow-up radiographs: 6 weeks, 3 months, 1 year, then biennial
- Implant surveillance: lifetime follow-up recommended

## 8. Non-Covered Services

- Bilateral simultaneous TKA in patients with BMI > 35, ASA class >= III, or age >= 80
- Knee arthroplasty performed solely for patellofemoral compartment disease without documented failure of conservative management specific to the patellofemoral joint
- Custom or patient-specific implants without documented clinical justification for deviation from standard off-the-shelf components
- Robotic-assisted surgery is considered a surgical approach, not a separately reimbursable service

## 9. Documentation Requirements

The requesting provider must submit:

1. History and physical examination documenting knee symptoms and functional limitations
2. Weight-bearing knee radiographs (AP, lateral, sunrise view) within the last 12 months
3. Conservative treatment history with dates, durations, and outcomes
4. BMI measurement from within the last 3 months
5. Validated outcome score (WOMAC, KOOS, or KSS) — recommended but not required
6. Operative plan including approach, implant type, and laterality
7. For diabetic patients: HbA1c within the last 3 months
