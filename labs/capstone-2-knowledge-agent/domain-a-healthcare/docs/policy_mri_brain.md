# Clinical Policy: Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) of the Brain

**Policy Number:** CP-RAD-2024-001
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2024
**Last Reviewed:** December 15, 2023
**Category:** Radiology — Advanced Imaging
**LOB:** Commercial, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid Managed Care

---

## 1. Policy Statement

This policy establishes the medical necessity criteria for Magnetic Resonance
Imaging (MRI) of the brain. MRI of the brain is considered medically necessary
when the clinical criteria outlined in this document are met. Prior
authorization is required for all outpatient brain MRI studies.

## 2. Applicable CPT Codes

| CPT Code | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| 70551 | MRI brain without contrast |
| 70552 | MRI brain with contrast |
| 70553 | MRI brain without contrast followed by with contrast |
| 70554 | MRI brain functional (fMRI) |
| 70555 | MRI brain functional requiring physician administration of stimuli |

## 3. Medical Necessity Criteria

### 3.1 Indications for MRI Brain Without Contrast (CPT 70551)

MRI of the brain without contrast is considered medically necessary for any of
the following indications:

**Headache:**
- New-onset severe headache with neurological deficits
- Thunderclap headache (sudden onset reaching maximum intensity within 60 seconds)
- Progressive headache pattern with papilledema
- New headache in patients over age 50 with no prior headache history
- Headache with fever and neck stiffness when meningitis is suspected
- Chronic headache (>3 months) that has changed in character, frequency, or severity AND has failed conservative management for at least 6 weeks

**Note:** Routine headache without red-flag symptoms does NOT meet medical
necessity criteria. Red-flag symptoms include: sudden onset, progressive
worsening, neurological deficits, fever, history of malignancy, history of
immunosuppression, age >50 with new-onset headache, or headache triggered by
Valsalva maneuver.

**Seizures:**
- New-onset seizure in any adult patient (age 18+)
- New-onset seizure in pediatric patient with focal features
- Breakthrough seizures in a patient with known epilepsy on stable medication
- Status epilepticus after stabilization
- Pre-surgical evaluation for refractory epilepsy (ICD-10: G40.x)

**Cognitive Decline / Dementia Evaluation:**
- Progressive cognitive decline documented over at least 6 months by validated cognitive assessment (MMSE, MoCA, or equivalent)
- Rapid cognitive decline (weeks to months) to rule out prion disease, autoimmune encephalitis, or other rapidly progressive conditions
- New-onset behavioral changes with cognitive symptoms in patients under age 65

**Stroke / Cerebrovascular:**
- Acute focal neurological deficit consistent with stroke (within 24 hours of onset)
- Transient ischemic attack (TIA) for evaluation of underlying etiology
- Known cerebrovascular disease with new or changing symptoms
- ICD-10 codes: I63.x (cerebral infarction), G45.x (TIA)

**Trauma:**
- Moderate to severe traumatic brain injury (GCS 3-12) when CT findings are inconclusive
- Mild TBI with persistent symptoms beyond 2 weeks
- Post-traumatic amnesia lasting more than 24 hours

### 3.2 Indications for MRI Brain With Contrast (CPT 70552)

MRI of the brain with gadolinium contrast is medically necessary for:

- Known or suspected primary brain neoplasm (ICD-10: C71.x, D33.x)
- Known or suspected brain metastases (ICD-10: C79.31)
- Surveillance imaging for treated brain tumor per NCCN guidelines
- Suspected intracranial infection (abscess, encephalitis, meningitis)
- Multiple sclerosis: initial diagnosis or monitoring disease activity (ICD-10: G35)
- Pituitary adenoma evaluation (ICD-10: D35.2)
- Suspected acoustic neuroma / vestibular schwannoma (ICD-10: D33.3)
- Cranial nerve palsy of unclear etiology

### 3.3 Indications for MRI Brain Without and With Contrast (CPT 70553)

Combined study (without contrast followed by with contrast) is medically
necessary when:

- Initial evaluation of suspected brain tumor where characterization of the lesion requires both sequences
- Evaluation of suspected demyelinating disease (multiple sclerosis) at initial presentation
- Post-operative brain tumor surveillance at the following intervals:
  - High-grade glioma (WHO Grade III-IV): every 2-3 months for the first 2 years, then every 3-6 months
  - Low-grade glioma (WHO Grade I-II): every 3-6 months for the first 5 years, then annually
  - Meningioma (post-surgical): 3 months post-op, then annually for 5 years

### 3.4 Functional MRI (CPT 70554, 70555)

Functional MRI is considered medically necessary ONLY for:

- Pre-surgical mapping of eloquent cortex prior to brain tumor resection
- Pre-surgical language lateralization for epilepsy surgery candidates
- Must be performed at a facility with documented fMRI expertise and qualified interpreting physician

## 4. Contraindications and Exclusions

### 4.1 Absolute Contraindications
- Non-MRI-conditional cardiac pacemaker or defibrillator
- Ferromagnetic intracranial aneurysm clips
- Metallic foreign body in the orbit
- Cochlear implants (unless specifically MRI-conditional)

### 4.2 Not Medically Necessary
- Screening MRI in asymptomatic patients without risk factors
- Routine follow-up of stable, benign findings (e.g., arachnoid cyst, stable white matter changes)
- Repeat MRI within 90 days of a prior brain MRI unless new symptoms have developed or there is a clinical change
- Headache that does not meet the red-flag criteria listed in Section 3.1
- Pre-employment or insurance-related screening
- Concussion without persistent symptoms beyond the acute phase (first 2 weeks)

## 5. Documentation Requirements

The ordering physician must provide:

1. **Clinical indication** — specific signs, symptoms, or diagnosis requiring imaging
2. **Relevant ICD-10 code(s)** — must correspond to an approved indication
3. **Duration and progression** — how long symptoms have been present and trajectory
4. **Prior imaging results** — results of any prior CT or MRI of the brain within the last 12 months
5. **Physical exam findings** — relevant neurological exam findings
6. **Failed conservative treatments** — for chronic headache, documentation of at least 6 weeks of conservative management

## 6. Peer-to-Peer Review Process

If the initial clinical review results in a non-authorization, the ordering
physician may request a peer-to-peer review:

- Must be requested within 5 business days of the non-authorization decision
- Review will be conducted by a board-certified radiologist or neurologist
- Additional clinical information may be submitted
- Decision will be rendered within 2 business days of the peer review

## 7. Appeal Rights

Members and providers may appeal a denial decision:

- **First-level appeal:** Must be filed within 60 days of the denial. Reviewed by a physician not involved in the original decision.
- **Second-level appeal:** Must be filed within 60 days of the first-level appeal decision. Reviewed by an external independent review organization (IRO).
- **Expedited appeal:** Available when delay could seriously jeopardize the member's health. Decision rendered within 72 hours.
