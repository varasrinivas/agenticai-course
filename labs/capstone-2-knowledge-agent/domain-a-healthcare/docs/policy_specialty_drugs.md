## 1. Policy Statement

This policy establishes prior authorization criteria for select biologic and
biosimilar medications classified as specialty drugs. Specialty drugs are
defined as medications that cost more than $1,000 per month, require special
handling (cold chain, restricted distribution), are administered via injection
or infusion, or require ongoing clinical monitoring.

All specialty drugs in this policy require prior authorization before initiation
and reauthorization at defined intervals.

## 3. General Prior Authorization Criteria

### 3.1 Initial Authorization (applies to ALL specialty drugs)

The following criteria must be met for initial authorization of any specialty
drug listed in this policy:

**A. Prescriber Requirements:**
- Must be prescribed by a specialist appropriate to the indication:
  - Rheumatology: rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis
  - Dermatology: psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, hidradenitis suppurativa
  - Gastroenterology: Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis
  - Neurology: multiple sclerosis
- Primary care prescribers may prescribe ONLY if co-managed with the appropriate specialist (documentation required)

**B. Diagnosis Confirmation:**
- ICD-10 code corresponding to an FDA-approved indication
- Clinical documentation supporting the diagnosis (labs, imaging, biopsy, or validated scoring tool as applicable)

**C. Baseline Testing (prior to initiation):**
- Tuberculosis (TB) screening: negative TB skin test (TST) or interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA) within 6 months
- Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) and Hepatitis B core antibody (anti-HBc)
- CBC with differential
- Hepatic function panel (AST, ALT, bilirubin)
- Age-appropriate cancer screening up to date

**D. Contraindication Screen:**
- No active serious infection (sepsis, pneumonia, active TB, opportunistic infection)
- No history of demyelinating disease (for TNF inhibitors)
- No active malignancy (within the last 5 years, excluding non-melanoma skin cancer)
- Not pregnant or planning pregnancy within 6 months (drug-specific; consult labeling)

### 3.2 Step Therapy Requirements

**TNF-Alpha Inhibitors:**
- New starts: Must trial a preferred biosimilar (Hadlima, Hyrimoz, or Cyltezo) before the reference product (Humira) will be authorized
- Exception: Patients stable on Humira for >= 12 months are exempt from biosimilar step therapy
- Documented failure (lack of efficacy after 12 weeks), intolerance, or contraindication to the biosimilar is required before authorization of the reference biologic

**Interleukin Inhibitors:**
- For psoriasis: Must have documented failure or intolerance to at least 1 TNF inhibitor AND at least 1 conventional systemic therapy (methotrexate, cyclosporine, or acitretin)
- For atopic dermatitis (Dupixent): Must have documented failure of at least 2 topical therapies (one being a mid-to-high potency topical corticosteroid) AND at least 1 systemic immunosuppressant (methotrexate, cyclosporine, mycophenolate, or azathioprine)

**MS Agents:**
- Ocrevus or Kesimpta: May be authorized as first-line for relapsing MS without step therapy requirement
- Tysabri: Reserved for patients who have failed at least 1 other MS disease-modifying therapy OR have highly active disease (2+ relapses in prior 12 months with MRI activity). JC virus antibody testing required before initiation and every 6 months thereafter.

### 3.4 Reauthorization Criteria

At each reauthorization interval, the following must be documented:

1. **Continued medical necessity** — disease remains active or requires ongoing suppression
2. **Clinical response** — documented improvement or stability using validated measures:
   - RA: DAS28 improvement >= 1.2 from baseline OR DAS28 <= 3.2 (low disease activity)
   - Psoriasis: PASI-75 response (75% improvement from baseline) OR BSA <= 3%
   - Crohn's/UC: Harvey-Bradshaw Index <= 4 (remission) or improvement from baseline
   - MS: No relapses, stable or improved MRI, stable EDSS score
3. **Medication adherence** — claims data showing >= 80% medication possession ratio (MPR)
4. **Safety monitoring** — periodic labs as clinically indicated (CBC, hepatic panel)
5. **Specialist follow-up** — at least 1 visit with the prescribing specialist in the authorization period

## 5. Site of Service Requirements

### 5.1 Provider-Administered Infusions (Medical Benefit)

For IV-administered specialty drugs (Remicade, Ocrevus, Tysabri):

- **Preferred site:** Physician office or hospital outpatient infusion center
- **Home infusion:** May be approved after at least 2 uneventful infusions at a clinical site. Not available for Tysabri (TOUCH program requires certified infusion center).
- **Hospital outpatient department (HOPD):** Approved for initial infusion or patients with history of infusion reactions. Step-down to physician office required after 3 uneventful infusions.

### 5.2 Self-Administered Injections (Pharmacy Benefit)

For subcutaneous specialty drugs:

- Dispensed through specialty pharmacy network only
- Patient must complete injection training with a healthcare professional
- Specialty pharmacy must provide patient support services (refill reminders, adherence support, cold chain management)

## 6. Coverage Exclusions

The following are NOT covered under this policy:

- Off-label use not supported by compendia (AHFS, DrugDex, NCCN) or peer-reviewed literature
- Use in combination with another biologic agent (e.g., TNF inhibitor + IL inhibitor simultaneously) — exception: rituximab may be used with methotrexate per standard RA protocols
- Exceeding FDA-approved maximum dosing without peer-reviewed evidence supporting dose escalation
- Continuation of therapy with documented clinical non-response after adequate trial (defined per Section 3.4)
- Specialty drugs obtained from non-network specialty pharmacies without prior coordination of benefits approval

## 7. Appeals and Exceptions

- **Clinical exception requests:** May be submitted when standard criteria are not met. Must include peer-reviewed literature supporting the off-criteria use, specialist letter of medical necessity, and documentation of prior treatment failures.
- **Formulary exception:** Required when requesting a non-preferred agent before trialing a preferred agent. Must document clinical rationale for why the preferred agent is not appropriate.
- **Urgent/expedited review:** Available for patients with acute disease flares or conditions that cannot safely wait for standard review timelines (decision within 24 hours).

## 8. Drug-Specific Clinical Criteria

### 8.1 Adalimumab (Humira) and Biosimilars — Rheumatoid Arthritis

- Diagnosis of moderate to severe RA (DAS28 > 3.2)
- Failed or intolerant to methotrexate (at least 15mg/week for 12 weeks) unless contraindicated
- If methotrexate contraindicated: document reason (hepatic disease, cytopenias, pregnancy planning, intolerance)
- Must continue background DMARD (methotrexate preferred) unless contraindicated
- Loading dose: 80mg (adalimumab only), then 40mg every other week

### 8.2 Dupilumab (Dupixent) — Atopic Dermatitis

- Age >= 6 months (per FDA approval)
- Moderate to severe atopic dermatitis defined by: EASI >= 16, BSA >= 10%, AND IGA >= 3
- Failed or intolerant to at least 2 topical therapies used for at least 4 weeks each
- For patients >= 18 years: failed or intolerant to at least 1 systemic immunosuppressant
- Loading dose: 600mg (two 300mg injections), then 300mg every other week
- Pediatric dosing: weight-based per FDA labeling

### 8.3 Ocrevus (ocrelizumab) — Multiple Sclerosis

- Diagnosis of relapsing MS (RMS) or primary progressive MS (PPMS) by McDonald criteria
- For RMS: at least 1 documented relapse in the prior 24 months OR active MRI lesions
- For PPMS: EDSS 3.0-6.5, disease duration < 15 years, elevated CSF IgG index or oligoclonal bands
- Initial dose: 300mg IV, followed by 300mg IV 14 days later
- Maintenance: 600mg IV every 6 months
- Hepatitis B screening required before initiation
- No live vaccines within 6 weeks of initiation
