QUERY 1:
  "What are the medical necessity criteria for a brain MRI without contrast?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  According to the clinical policy, MRI of the brain without contrast
  (CPT 70551) is considered medically necessary for the following
  indications:

  1. **Headache** -- new-onset severe headache with neurological deficits,
     thunderclap headache, progressive headache with papilledema, new headache
     in patients over age 50, headache with fever and neck stiffness, or
     chronic headache (>3 months) that has changed in character and has
     failed conservative management for at least 6 weeks.
     [Source: policy_mri_brain.md, Chunk 2]

  2. **Seizures** -- new-onset seizure in adults, new-onset seizure in
     pediatric patients with focal features, breakthrough seizures on stable
     medication, status epilepticus after stabilization, or pre-surgical
     evaluation for refractory epilepsy.
     [Source: policy_mri_brain.md, Chunk 3]

  3. **Cognitive Decline** -- progressive cognitive decline documented over
     at least 6 months, rapid cognitive decline, or new-onset behavioral
     changes with cognitive symptoms in patients under 65.
     [Source: policy_mri_brain.md, Chunk 3]

  4. **Stroke / Cerebrovascular** -- acute focal neurological deficit, TIA
     evaluation, or known cerebrovascular disease with new symptoms.
     [Source: policy_mri_brain.md, Chunk 4]

  5. **Trauma** -- moderate to severe TBI when CT is inconclusive, mild TBI
     with persistent symptoms beyond 2 weeks, or post-traumatic amnesia
     lasting more than 24 hours.
     [Source: policy_mri_brain.md, Chunk 4]

  Note: Routine headache without red-flag symptoms does NOT meet criteria.

QUERY 2:
  "What conservative treatments are required before a total knee replacement
   can be authorized?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  The policy requires documentation of trial and failure or intolerance of
  at least 3 of the following 5 conservative treatments, over a minimum
  of 3 months:

  1. **Pharmacotherapy** -- trial of at least 2 agents: acetaminophen (up to
     3000mg/day for at least 4 weeks), oral NSAIDs (at least 2 different
     agents, each for at least 4 weeks), topical NSAIDs (at least 4 weeks),
     or duloxetine (for NSAID contraindications).
     [Source: policy_knee_replacement.md, Chunk 4]

  2. **Physical Therapy** -- supervised PT program with a minimum of 6 visits
     over at least 6 weeks, including quadriceps strengthening and ROM
     exercises, with documentation of attendance and progress.
     [Source: policy_knee_replacement.md, Chunk 4]

  3. **Intra-articular Injections** -- at least 1 corticosteroid injection
     (max 3/year) or completed hyaluronic acid viscosupplementation series.
     [Source: policy_knee_replacement.md, Chunk 5]

  4. **Activity Modification and Weight Management** -- documented counseling
     and weight loss counseling or program referral if BMI >= 30.
     [Source: policy_knee_replacement.md, Chunk 5]

  5. **Assistive Devices** -- trial of unloader knee brace, cane, or walker
     with documentation of compliance and inadequate symptom relief.
     [Source: policy_knee_replacement.md, Chunk 5]

QUERY 3:
  "Does the specialty drug policy require step therapy for adalimumab?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  Yes. For new starts on a TNF-alpha inhibitor, the policy requires patients
  to trial a preferred biosimilar (Hadlima, Hyrimoz, or Cyltezo) before the
  reference product Humira (adalimumab) will be authorized. Documented
  failure (lack of efficacy after 12 weeks), intolerance, or contraindication
  to the biosimilar is required before the reference biologic is authorized.
  [Source: policy_specialty_drugs.md, Chunk 5]

  However, patients who are already stable on Humira for 12 months or more
  are exempt from the biosimilar step therapy requirement.
  [Source: policy_specialty_drugs.md, Chunk 5]

QUERY 4:
  "How many physical therapy visits are covered after ACL reconstruction?"

EXPECTED RESPONSE:
  According to the rehabilitation policy, ACL reconstruction is eligible for
  up to 36 visits over 6 months, following a phased protocol:

  - Phase 1 (Weeks 0-2): ROM, quad activation, gait training
  - Phase 2 (Weeks 2-6): Progressive strengthening, balance training
  - Phase 3 (Weeks 6-12): Functional training, sport-specific activities
  - Phase 4 (Months 3-6): Return-to-sport progression, agility, plyometrics
  [Source: policy_physical_therapy.md, Chunk 8]

  The first 12 visits within 8 weeks of surgery do not require prior
  authorization (Tier 1). Continuation beyond 12 visits requires concurrent
  review with submission of treatment plan, progress notes, and functional
  outcome data.
  [Source: policy_physical_therapy.md, Chunk 4]
