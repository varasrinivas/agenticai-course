# UCC Filing Types Guide

## Overview

The Uniform Commercial Code establishes several standardized filing forms that secured parties use to create, modify, and terminate public records of their security interests. Understanding each filing type is essential for anyone conducting due diligence, managing collateral portfolios, or working in commercial lending.

All UCC filings are submitted to the appropriate state filing office, typically the Secretary of State. Each state provides standardized forms, though the content requirements are governed by Article 9 of the UCC.

## UCC-1: Initial Financing Statement

The UCC-1 is the foundational filing. It creates the public record that a secured party claims a security interest in the debtor's property. Filing a UCC-1 is the most common method of perfecting a security interest under Article 9.

### Required Information

A valid UCC-1 must contain three pieces of information:

1. **Debtor's legal name.** For organizations, this must exactly match the name on the formation documents (articles of incorporation, certificate of formation, etc.). For individuals, it must match the name on the debtor's unexpired driver's license. Errors in the debtor's name are the single most common cause of defective filings.

2. **Secured party's name and address.** The name of the creditor claiming the security interest. If an assignee is involved, the assignee's information should also be included.

3. **Collateral description.** A description of the property subject to the security interest. The description can be specific ("all inventory located at 123 Main St") or broad ("all assets"). Supergeneric descriptions like "all assets" or "all personal property" are permitted on financing statements, unlike in security agreements where more specificity may be required for certain collateral types.

### When to File

File a UCC-1 whenever you are taking a security interest in personal property and need to perfect by filing. This is required for most types of collateral, including accounts, equipment, inventory, general intangibles, and most goods.

### Duration and Lapse

A UCC-1 is effective for five years from the date of filing. If no continuation statement is filed, the financing statement lapses at the end of the five-year period, and the security interest becomes unperfected. The lapse is automatic and requires no action by any party.

### Common Mistakes

- Misspelling the debtor's name or using a trade name instead of the legal name
- Filing in the wrong state (should be debtor's state of organization, not where collateral is located)
- Using an overly vague collateral description that does not put searchers on notice
- Failing to calendar the five-year expiration date

## UCC-3: Financing Statement Amendment

The UCC-3 is a multipurpose amendment form used to modify, continue, terminate, or assign an existing UCC-1 filing. It references the original filing by its file number and can serve four distinct functions.

### Continuation

A continuation statement extends the effectiveness of a UCC-1 for an additional five years. It must be filed within six months before the current expiration date. Filing a continuation too early (more than six months before expiration) or too late (after expiration) is ineffective. Missing the continuation window is a critical and surprisingly common error that results in loss of perfection.

### Amendment

An amendment modifies the information in the original filing. Common amendments include adding or deleting collateral, changing the debtor's name after a legal name change, or updating the secured party's information. If a debtor changes its legal name and the change is so significant that a search under the new name would not reveal the original filing, the secured party must file an amendment within four months or risk losing perfection in after-acquired collateral.

### Termination

A termination statement indicates that the secured party no longer claims a security interest under the filing. Under Revised Article 9, if the debtor is a consumer and there is no outstanding obligation, the secured party must file a termination within one month of demand or 20 days of receiving an authenticated demand. For non-consumer transactions, the secured party must send a termination statement to the debtor, who may then file it.

Failing to file a timely termination can expose the secured party to liability, including statutory damages in some jurisdictions.

### Assignment

An assignment transfers the secured party's interest under the filing to a new party. This is common when loans are sold on the secondary market. The UCC-3 assignment records the new secured party of record and ensures that lien searches reflect the current holder of the security interest.

## UCC-5: Correction Statement

The UCC-5 is a correction statement (sometimes called an "information statement") that allows a debtor or other person to place a notice on the public record that a filed financing statement is inaccurate or was wrongfully filed.

### Important Limitation

A UCC-5 does not affect the legal effectiveness of the financing statement it references. It is purely informational. Even after a UCC-5 is filed, the original financing statement remains legally effective. The UCC-5 simply provides notice to searchers that the filing is disputed.

### When to Use

- The debtor believes a financing statement was filed without authorization
- The collateral description is materially inaccurate
- The debtor's name is incorrect on the filing
- The filing was not terminated after the obligation was satisfied

### Filing Requirements

The UCC-5 must identify the filing it relates to (by file number), state the basis for the correction, and be filed in the same office where the original financing statement was filed. There is no requirement for the secured party to consent to the filing of a UCC-5.

### Practical Impact

While a UCC-5 has no legal effect on the underlying filing, it serves as a red flag to anyone searching the record. Lenders and buyers conducting due diligence should investigate any UCC-5 filings thoroughly, as they may indicate disputes about the validity or scope of a security interest.
