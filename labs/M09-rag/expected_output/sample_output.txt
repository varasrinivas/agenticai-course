Question: What is the difference between a UCC-1 and UCC-3 filing?
============================================================

  Sources retrieved:
    1. ucc-filing-types.md — UCC-1: Initial Financing Statement (distance: 0.5234)
    2. ucc-filing-types.md — UCC-3: Financing Statement Amendment (distance: 0.5891)
    3. ucc-filing-types.md — Overview (distance: 0.7123)

Answer:
A UCC-1 and UCC-3 serve different but complementary purposes in the UCC filing system:

**UCC-1 (Initial Financing Statement):** This is the foundational filing that creates the
public record of a security interest. It must contain the debtor's legal name, the secured
party's name, and a description of the collateral. A UCC-1 is effective for five years from
filing and is the primary method of perfecting a security interest. [Source: ucc-filing-types.md]

**UCC-3 (Financing Statement Amendment):** This is a multipurpose form used to modify an
existing UCC-1 filing. It can serve four functions: (1) continuation — extending the filing
for another five years, (2) amendment — changing debtor name, collateral, or party info,
(3) termination — indicating the secured party no longer claims the interest, and
(4) assignment — transferring the interest to a new party. [Source: ucc-filing-types.md]

In short, the UCC-1 creates the filing, while the UCC-3 modifies, extends, terminates,
or assigns it.

Question: What are the priority rules for secured transactions?
============================================================

  Sources retrieved:
    1. ucc-article-9-overview.md — Priority Rules (distance: 0.3892)
    2. ucc-article-9-overview.md — Key Concepts (distance: 0.8234)
    3. ucc-article-9-overview.md — Perfection (distance: 0.9012)

Answer:
Article 9 establishes several priority rules that determine which creditor gets paid first
when multiple parties claim a security interest in the same collateral:

1. **Perfected vs. Unperfected:** A perfected security interest almost always has priority
   over an unperfected one. [Source: ucc-article-9-overview.md]

2. **First to File or Perfect:** Among perfected interests, priority goes to the party that
   first filed or first perfected, whichever was earlier. [Source: ucc-article-9-overview.md]

3. **PMSI Superpriority:** A purchase-money security interest (PMSI) can have priority over
   earlier filings if properly perfected — within 20 days for goods, or before delivery plus
   notice for inventory. [Source: ucc-article-9-overview.md]

4. **Buyers in Ordinary Course:** Buyers who purchase goods in the ordinary course of business
   take free of security interests created by the seller. [Source: ucc-article-9-overview.md]

5. **Lien Creditors:** A judicial lien creditor defeats unperfected interests but loses to
   interests perfected before the lien arises. [Source: ucc-article-9-overview.md]

Question: How should I interpret multiple liens on the same debtor?
============================================================

  Sources retrieved:
    1. lien-search-guide.md — Red Flags (distance: 0.4567)
    2. lien-search-guide.md — Interpreting Results (distance: 0.5123)
    3. ucc-article-9-overview.md — Priority Rules (distance: 0.7891)

Answer:
Multiple liens on the same debtor should be interpreted carefully as part of your due
diligence process:

When several creditors hold security interests in the same debtor's assets, it indicates
the debtor has leveraged its collateral to obtain multiple lines of credit. While this is
not inherently problematic, it increases credit risk. You should pay close attention to
the priority ordering of the filings and determine whether your proposed security interest
would be junior to existing ones. [Source: lien-search-guide.md]

For each filing, examine the secured party, collateral description, filing date, status,
and any amendments. Earlier filings generally have priority, so understanding the timeline
is critical. [Source: lien-search-guide.md]

Under Article 9's priority rules, among perfected security interests, priority generally
goes to the party that first filed or perfected. [Source: ucc-article-9-overview.md]

Question: What is a blanket lien and why is it a red flag?
============================================================

  Sources retrieved:
    1. lien-search-guide.md — Red Flags (distance: 0.3456)
    2. lien-search-guide.md — Interpreting Results (distance: 0.6789)
    3. ucc-filing-types.md — UCC-1: Initial Financing Statement (distance: 0.8234)

Answer:
A blanket lien covers "all assets" or "all personal property" of the debtor, meaning the
secured party claims a security interest in everything the debtor owns.
[Source: lien-search-guide.md]

It is considered a red flag because a prior blanket lien makes it extremely difficult for
a subsequent lender to obtain a meaningful security position. Since the prior lien holder
has first priority on all assets, any new lender would be in a junior position on every
piece of collateral. If you discover a blanket lien during due diligence, you should
determine whether the existing lender will subordinate its interest or carve out specific
collateral for your transaction. [Source: lien-search-guide.md]
