CALL 1: Basic — No System Prompt
============================================================
A UCC-1 filing is a legal document filed under the Uniform Commercial Code
(UCC) that establishes a creditor's security interest in a debtor's personal
property. When a lender provides a loan secured by personal property (as
opposed to real estate), they file a UCC-1 financing statement with the
appropriate state office to publicly declare their claim on that collateral.

============================================================
CALL 2: With System Prompt (UCC Expert)
============================================================
A UCC-1 financing statement is the foundational document in secured
transactions under Article 9 of the Uniform Commercial Code. It serves as
a public notice filed with the state Secretary of State (or equivalent
filing office) to perfect a security interest in a debtor's personal
property or fixtures.

Key elements of a UCC-1 filing:
- Debtor name and address
- Secured party name and address
- Description of collateral
- Filing date and jurisdiction

The filing establishes priority among competing creditors — the first to
file generally has first claim on the collateral in case of default.

## Analysis

A **UCC-1 financing statement** is a legal form filed to perfect a security
interest under Article 9 of the Uniform Commercial Code.

**Purpose:** The filing creates a public record that a lender (secured party)
has a claim on specific assets of a borrower (debtor). This "perfection"
establishes the lender's priority over other creditors.

**Key Components:**
1. **Debtor information** — legal name and mailing address
2. **Secured party information** — the creditor's details
3. **Collateral description** — what assets secure the loan

**Duration:** A standard UCC-1 filing is effective for 5 years from the
filing date. It can be extended by filing a UCC-3 continuation statement
before expiration.

Multi-Turn Conversation
============================================================

--- Turn 1 ---
USER: What is a UCC-1 filing?
CLAUDE: A UCC-1 filing is a financing statement filed under the Uniform
Commercial Code to publicly establish a creditor's security interest in a
debtor's personal property. It's essentially a public notice that says
"this lender has a claim on these specific assets." The filing is made with
the state Secretary of State's office and creates a public record that other
creditors and interested parties can search.

--- Turn 2 ---
USER: How long do they last?
CLAUDE: A standard UCC-1 financing statement is effective for 5 years from
the date of filing. Before that 5-year period expires, the secured party
can file a UCC-3 continuation statement to extend it for another 5 years.
There's no limit on how many times you can continue a filing, but you must
file the continuation before the current period lapses — typically within
the last 6 months before expiration.

--- Turn 3 ---
USER: What happens when one expires?
CLAUDE: When a UCC-1 filing expires (lapses) without a continuation being
filed, several things happen:

1. The security interest becomes unperfected — the creditor loses their
   priority position against other creditors
2. The filing office removes the record from active search results
3. Other creditors who file after the lapse would take priority
4. The underlying debt still exists, but the creditor's secured position
   is lost

This is why lenders have systems to track expiration dates — letting a
filing lapse can be a costly mistake, especially if the debtor has other
creditors or enters bankruptcy.
